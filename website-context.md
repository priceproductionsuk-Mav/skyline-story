# Website Context — Production & Media Installations Company

Everything decided so far for the company website. Use this as the brief when building. Where something is **not decided yet**, it is marked as open — don't invent an answer; leave a clear placeholder.

Last updated: 9 October 2026

---

## 1. The company

- First arm of a pre-launch global media corporation. It designs and installs professional production systems: audio and sound desks, stages, lighting, video, and livestream.
- Offering: turnkey installation, plus training the client's own team to run it, plus ongoing maintenance.
- Not church-coded. The first target market is a network of ~200 London churches, but the brand must read as a serious, global production company.
- **Company name: not decided.** The leading candidate is "SkylineStory" (not locked). Use `[COMPANY NAME]` as a placeholder everywhere.
- **Logo: none.** Don't design or include one. Use a plain text placeholder for the name.

---

## 2. Brand and visual direction

- **Feel:** Apple-like, premium, calm and confident. Restraint over decoration. Generous empty space, especially on desktop: don't fill the width.
- **Colours:** dark/black background; silver and white for text and lines; warm amber/soft gold for the light ("signals") travelling along the lines, like a lighting rig warming up.
- **Avoid:** clinical blues, whites-on-white and pinks (anything that reads as medical); bouncy or flashy motion.
- **Motion:** smooth and unhurried, soft easing, never snapping.
- **Depth:** subtle. A gentle shadow or a faint frosted-glass feel on cards against the dark.
- **Type:** one or two typefaces, well spaced. (Specific fonts are not chosen yet.)
- **The animation is the one dramatic moment.** Every other section stays quiet and clean so it doesn't compete.

### Copy rules
- Short, snappy lines, each one a "jab", but eloquent and professional.
- Every line points at the client's result, not at what we do.
- No word or phrase repeats across sections. Lines must not echo each other.
- Never use "mind", "brain" or "neuron" in site copy. Use the vocabulary of connection, signal, flow and light.

## 3. Page structure (single scrolling page, in this order)

1. **Intro**: Why → How → What lines, with the dots-and-lines animation forming a stage
2. **Client process**: the 5 steps (dots from the stage become the step markers)
3. **Services**: 4 services
4. **Packages**: 4 packages
5. **Questionnaire**: where visitors act (it is also step 1 of the process)
6. **Contact**: final section

---

## 4. Intro

Three one-liners appear one at a time as the visitor scrolls. Each holds the screen on its own before the next arrives. The order is fixed: Why, then How, then What.

| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| **Why** | Every voice deserves to carry. Every moment deserves to land. |
| **How** | Engineered for effortless operation. |
| **What** | Professional audio, lighting, video and livestream. |

Services are not explained in the intro.

### Scroll animation: the only concept

Dots and lines run down the page behind the text, with light travelling along the lines, and they resolve into a stage at the end. **All other animation ideas are scrapped** (no knot, no brain or mind image).

The animation runs in three beats, synced to the three lines:

- **Why:** dots scattered on black, with loose, crossing lines. Amber light starts along a line and fades out before it arrives (signals failing).
- **How:** the dots connect into clean lines. Light now travels end to end without breaking.
- **What:** the lines converge onto the key points of a stage outline (a platform with a rig or screen above it). The light runs through it and the stage **switches on**: the rig glows and the screen wakes. This is the final beat.

Notes:
- The first scattered dots are already drifting behind the screen on page load.
- The stage should emerge from the connections, not morph in from nothing.
- Desktop: the network spreads across the full width, for a more cinematic effect.

## 5. Client process (5 steps)

**Animation link:** once the stage has formed, five of its dots break away and travel down the page, and each one settles beside a step as that step's marker. A thin line joins the five dots. Light travels along it and lights each dot as its step scrolls into view.

Each step is a number, a short title and one line:

| # | Title | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Questionnaire | Tell us what you need. |
| 2 | Audit | We assess your room. |
| 3 | Build | Designed for your room. |
| 4 | Training | Anyone can run it. |
| 5 | Running and maintained | It keeps working. |

What happens at each step, for context:
1. The client fills out the relevant questionnaire (existing-system audit or new-system build).
2. A meeting with the client to audit the existing system, or to assess what's needed for a new one.
3. Freelancers are brought in to build the system.
4. The client's team is trained to run it.
5. The system is up and running, with maintenance on an ongoing basis.

The audit must stay at step 2, before the build.

Desktop layout is **open**: the steps could run horizontally, or the lit stage could stay pinned to one side while the steps scroll past.

## 6. Services (4)

Each service is a small card on the dark background: a short bold title and one line. A small node/dot motif keeps the cards visually part of the network.

| Title | Line |
|---|---|
| Design and installation | Built around your space. |
| Upgrades | Sharper sound, light and stream. |
| Audit and problem solving | We find it. We fix it. |
| Training and maintenance | Run with confidence, kept running. |

Full service names, for reference: production systems design and installation; audio, lighting, video and livestream upgrades; existing system audit and problem solving; training and maintenance support.

- **Desktop:** four cards in a row.
- **Mobile:** single column.

## 7. Packages (4)

The cards fade in as the visitor scrolls. Each card has: the package number and name, a short description, team size, what's included, then a **"Price on Request"** button with **"Tailored to you"** on one line underneath. "Tailored to you" must fit on one line on every screen size. No prices are shown anywhere.

| Package | Description | Team size | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small system | Simple yet effective. Easy to assemble and disassemble | Around 2 people | Full design, installation and training for your team; maintenance available on request |
| Medium system | Effective and dynamic | 4 to 6 people | Full design, installation and training for your team, plus a maintenance package |
| Large system | Dynamic and excellent | 6 to 8 people | Full design, installation and training for your team, plus a maintenance package |
| Custom package | Built to the size of your organisation | No fixed team size | The full process: design, installation, training and a maintenance package |

- The "Price on Request" button leads to the questionnaire.
- **Desktop:** four cards across in a line, so they can be compared at a glance.
- **Mobile:** a swipeable, right-to-left carousel instead of stacking. The edge of the next card peeks in at the side, with position dots underneath. Very Apple-like and premium: the card glides and settles with a soft ease.

## 8. Questionnaire

It sits after Packages, as the point where visitors act.

### Branch first
One question with two buttons: **"Do you have a system already, or are you starting fresh?"** The choice sends visitors down one of two paths, so they only see the questions relevant to them.

### Path A: existing system (audit and fix)
1. Overview of the equipment currently in use
2. The issues or challenges they're experiencing
3. Size of the team operating it
4. Preferred timeframe for the audit
5. Rough budget (pick a range)
6. Contact details

### Path B: new system (build)
1. What the system is intended for
2. Their specific requirements
3. Size of the team that will operate it
4. Rough budget (pick a range), with "What matters most: getting started simply, or a full professional setup?"
5. Contact details

### Budget ranges (both paths)
Pick a range; no free-text number. The floor is **£1,000** and there is nothing below it.
- £1,000 – £2,500
- £2,500 – £5,000
- £5,000 – £10,000
- £10,000+

Suggested framing line above the ranges (not confirmed): "Quality systems start from around £1,000."

### Look and feel
- Same dark, restrained style.
- Questions are revealed a step at a time (one or two per screen) with a light progress indicator, so it feels like a conversation, not a form.
- Maximum of five or six questions per path.

## 10. Navigation

- **Hamburger menu only.** A single menu icon sits in the top corner, and the links stay hidden until it's tapped or clicked. This applies on both desktop and mobile.
- The navigation is **invisible over the intro**, to keep the animation pure, and fades in once the visitor has scrolled past it.
- Links map one-to-one to page sections and smooth-scroll to them (anchors, no separate pages): Process, Services, Packages, Questionnaire, Contact.
- **Open:** whether a "Get a Quote" button stays visible on its own outside the hamburger.

---

## 11. Desktop vs mobile

- **Mobile:** one clean column you thumb through. The packages are a swipe carousel.
- **Desktop:** the same journey, but wider, calmer and more cinematic. Related items sit side by side: services in a row, packages across. Leave big, confident empty space.
